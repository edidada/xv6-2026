Sv39 divides a virtual address into three 9-bit page-table indexes and a
12-bit offset. Valid non-leaf entries point to another page-table page.
V means valid, R/W/X control reads/writes/execution, U permits user access,
A records access, and D records writes. Physical addresses depend on each
run; the Actions pgtbltest transcript is the source of actual PTE values.

Low virtual pages contain the ELF text (user-readable/executable), data
and BSS (user-readable/writable), a stack guard (U clear), and the user
stack (U/R/W). Unallocated gaps have no valid PTE. Allocation size and
link layout determine how many low pages appear in print_pgtbl.

The last three pages below MAXVA are USYSCALL at MAXVA-3*4096 (U/R,
the process PID), TRAPFRAME at MAXVA-2*4096 (supervisor R/W, saved user
registers), and TRAMPOLINE at MAXVA-4096 (supervisor R/X, trap entry and
return instructions). Their U restrictions prevent users from modifying
saved trap state or entering the trampoline directly. vmprint walks all
valid levels; its leaf entries correspond to the entries returned by
pgpte, while its intermediate entries describe page-table storage.

uptime could also use a shared read-only page updated by timer interrupts.
Unlike a PID, ticks change and require a defined atomic read/update
protocol. A parent PID would need updates on reparenting.

Two-MiB pages are level-1 leaves with aligned virtual and physical bases.
Fork copies their contents into new superpages. Partial unmapping creates
a level-0 table over the same physical pages, preserving the retained
contents. The allocator tracks each constituent page so a demoted region
is reusable as a superpage once all 512 constituent pages are free.
